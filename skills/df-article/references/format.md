# Grammar of the df.tips/t/2725 Format

Sample: https://df.tips/t/2725; local copy — [2725.md](2725.md) (post body without the Discourse header).  
This document specifies structural mechanics rather than domain vocabulary; `§…` references point to the local copy.  
Copying phrasing from the sample is pointless because it analyzes a different website.  

## 1. Document Structure

1. Two parts: `# I. Analysis` (facts and inferences) and `# II. Recommendations` (actions).  
Nothing exists outside these two parts: no introduction, executive summary, methodology section, or findings overview.  
1. Every paragraph is addressable: a numbered heading followed immediately on the next line by `<a name='§P.N'></a>`; an existing anchor format without `§` is preserved during edits.  
Heading level equals the numbering depth plus one, and sibling numbers run consecutively starting from `1` inside their parent.  
Numbers, anchors, and link labels are generated by `df_article.py render`; do not type them manually.  
1. A heading is a bare number.  
A label after the number is permitted only on a parent grouping of homogeneous items and states their count (`§I.12.1`, `§I.26`).  
1. A parent with no body text is a pure grouping (`§I.2`).  
A parent with a single-line body states a general claim whose children provide the evidence or specific cases (`§I.2.1` → `§I.2.1.1`).  

## 2. Paragraph Types

Every paragraph belongs to exactly one type.  

1. **Observation.**  
A single statement with the key value in bold, a colon, a command the reader can reproduce, and its literal output in a blockquote (`§I.1`, `§I.4.1`).  
A negative observation ends with `<empty response>` (`§I.2.1.1`).  
Complex extraction is published as a script at a permanent URL and invoked in a single readable line (`§I.11.1`, `§I.12`) so the reader can scan the command at a glance.  
1. **Equivalence.**  
Multiple commands producing identical outputs, followed by a single sentence stating what that equivalence proves (`§I.3.1`).  
Stating a hash or body size in prose does not substitute for showing the commands.  
1. **Documented Fact.**  
A statement paired with a link to the exact section of the primary documentation including its anchor; link label format is `**domain**/path#anchor` (`§I.2.2`).  
Source wording is quoted inline as «*…*» (`§I.9.1`) or in a `>` blockquote with the link on the line below (`§I.11.3`).  
1. **Client Console Data.**  
A statement with a number and a link to the exact report including property ID and filters (`§I.21.1`, `§I.24.1`, `§I.29`).  
1. **Inference.**  
`**[§X](#I.X)** → conclusion` or `**{[§X](#I.X), [§Y](#I.Y)}** → conclusion`; chained arrows `→ … →` are permitted (`§I.5.2`).  
The arrow itself means `"therefore"`: never follow it with `because`, `since`, or `although`, and never restate the premise after the arrow.  
An inference states a property of the system, not how important the finding is.  
1. **Qualified Inference.**  
Uncertainty is stated inside the proposition itself and rests on an explicit premise: `"is the probable source"` (`§I.24.3`).  
Empirical verification by the author is stated in the first person: `"I established this behavior empirically"` (`§I.17.3`).  
There are no `"Limitation:"` blocks, plausibility percentages, or `"arguments for/against"` lists in the article.  
1. **Evaluation.**  
Permitted only as a direct inference from enumerated premises (`§I.14.3`: `"pointless"` backed by two child premise items).  
1. **Recommendation.**  
An imperative verb is either the very first word or the first word immediately after `**[§I.N](#I.N)** →`.  
Linking to a Part I premise is mandatory (`§II.4`, `§II.9`); implementation steps are child items linking to tool documentation (`§II.3.1`–`§II.3.5`).  
Conditional recommendation: `If X → do Y` (`§II.12.2`).  
Condition `X` must reference a preceding measurement or profiling step in Part II (`§II.12.1`); trailing subjunctive caveats appended after the imperative are prohibited.  

## 3. Sentence Mechanics

1. One sentence per line; a paragraph contains one to three sentences.  
1. Use concrete physical verbs: `runs`, `returns`, `contains`, `belongs`, `redirects`, `sends`, `excludes`.  
1. Format numbers and dates in bold; enumerated parts must sum to the stated total (`53 = 15 + 26 + 12` in `§I.12`).  
1. Wrap UI labels in `«»`, and code or identifiers in backticks.  
Include only as many identifiers in a sentence as the claim requires.  
1. Bold highlights the key value or premise link, never a lead-in label before a colon.
